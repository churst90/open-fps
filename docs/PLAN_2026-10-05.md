## 1. Roles

### What exists

Every server command passes one gate (`CommandHandler.Execute`, `Permissions.Table`). A permission is
a command's name, plus `fire-any`, `join-private`, `edit-any` and `move-player`. A role is a set of
permissions; Admin has all of them. Custom roles and single grants exist (`/role`, `/grant`,
`/revoke`). The four built-in roles today:

- **Player**: talking, moving, items, doors, vehicles, friends, teams, profile.
- **Moderator**: announce, motd, where, tp, bring, kick, mute, join private maps.
- **Developer**: announce, motd, where, tp, join private maps, give, fire-any, every building verb,
  the sound tools, savemap, edit-any.
- **Admin**: everything, plus accounts, roles, grants, moving other players, sessions.

### What is wrong with it

- Maps have an owner and a public flag (`MapData.OwnerId`, `IsPublic`), but only `/join` reads them.
  Nothing stops a developer building on, or saving, someone else's private map, and nothing lets a
  player build on their own. There is no command to make a map, claim one, or make it public or
  private.
- Players cannot do anything on their own map that they cannot do on a public one. That is the
  separation the game needs most.
- `/tp` and `/move` are the same command. Moving yourself by coordinates is a staff tool; a
  teleporter is a game item. They should be two things.
- `/perms NAME`, `/kick` and `/mute` check "is admin" directly instead of a permission, so a custom
  role cannot be given them.
- Only the admin can grant. Cody wants developers to grant too, never more than they have.
- `/give` can hand out anything, including what will be premium items.
- `/undo` covers only `/put`, and only until you disconnect.
- `CommandHandler.cs` is 2,455 lines holding moderation, accounts, building and inventory together.

### Recommendation

Four default roles are worth having, because they are four different jobs: playing, looking after
people, building the world, and owning the server. Ownership of a map is not a fifth role; it is a
scope. Every building permission applies either on maps you own, or on any map.

| | Player | Moderator | Developer | Admin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Play, chat, items, doors, vehicles, friends, teams | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| `/tp` with a teleporter in your inventory | yes | yes | yes | yes, without one |
| Build, `/spawn`, `/move` yourself, save, on maps you own | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Make a private map; make it public or private; invite | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Kick, mute, bring, where, announce, motd, warn, ban | no | yes | no | yes |
| Join private maps | no | yes (to answer reports) | yes | yes |
| Build, `/spawn`, `/move` yourself, save, on any map | no | no | yes | yes |
| Spawn NPCs and vehicles | own maps | own maps | any map | any map |
| Give ordinary items (guns, tools, ammo) | no | no | yes | yes |
| Give premium items (teleporter, vehicles) | no | no | no | yes |
| Grant permissions | no | no | only ones they have, only to players | yes |
| Move other players | no | bring only | no | yes |
| Accounts, roles, sessions, restart | no | no | no | yes |
| Admin gun | no | no | no | yes |

Each person's permissions can still be raised or lowered one at a time with `/grant` and `/revoke`;
the table is the starting point.

The rule for granting: a developer may grant a player a permission only if the developer holds it,
may never grant to a moderator, developer or admin, and may never grant `grant` itself. The admin
can do anything.

## 2. Teleporter, giving, sword, vehicles, admin gun

### Teleporter

Status: built (`OpenFPS.Server/Core/Teleporter.cs`, `OpenFPS.Common/TeleporterSounds.cs`, prefab
`teleporter`). Cody chose unlimited uses. It works from the hands or the back; Admin has `tp-free`.

`/tp` without a teleporter says "You don't have a teleporter." With one in your inventory it takes
you to a player, a named place or a saved spot. `/move` stays the staff command for coordinates.

What it is: a handheld device about the size of a phone, half a kilogram, carried in a pocket. It
does not need to look like anything; its description says what it is. Its sounds, modelled as
physical events:

- **Charging**: a rising whine like a camera flash charging (a small transformer and capacitor),
  about two seconds, so you and anyone near hear it coming.
- **Leaving**: the air where your body was rushes in to fill about 75 litres of space: a short low
  thump, heard by people where you left.
- **Arriving**: the same volume of air pushed out of the way where you appear: a sharper pop, heard
  by people where you arrive.
- **Ready**: a small tone when it can be used again.

**Cody**: unlimited uses, or charges (and a cooldown)? Can it take you to a player without their
consent?

### Giving

Status: built (`OpenFPS.Common/HandOverSounds.cs`).

A gift already arrives as speech: "admin gave you 1 teleporter. In your hands." It plays no sound.
Each item gets a hand-over sound from what it is: the teleporter's ready tone, a gun's action, keys
jingling, a sword in its scabbard. That sound plays at the receiver along with the line.

### Giving vehicles

Status: built. `/give NAME vehicle PRESET` (`give-premium`), `/spawn vehicle`, `/spawn helicopter`
(parked; still not flyable), `/spawn train`.

Nothing can give or spawn a vehicle today; vehicles come only from the map file. Planned:
`/give NAME car PRESET` parks one of that kind beside the receiver, owned by them. Helicopters can
be given the same way, but nobody can fly one yet: aircraft only fly their circuits. Flying a
helicopter needs its own controls (collective, cyclic, pedals mapped to keys) and is a project of
its own. No jets: the only jet is the airliner.

Spawning for admins and developers: `/spawn walker`, `/spawn car PRESET`, `/spawn bus`,
`/spawn helicopter`; trains only on a track that exists (`/spawn train PRESET` puts it on the
nearest track).

### Sword

Yes. An "Iron Sword" item exists but nothing can swing it; there is no melee code at all. The sword
can be modelled like the doors: the blade as a steel bar ringing in its own modes, the draw as
friction along the scabbard's throat, the swing as air shedding off the blade (louder the faster,
roughly the sixth power of speed), and strikes as blade against blade, wood, stone, metal or a body.
Melee itself (swing, reach, block, hit) comes with it.

## 3. Experience and badges

Nothing exists yet: no XP, levels or stats anywhere in the code.

### The rule

You earn a badge by doing the thing often and doing it well, not by playing a lot. Every action that
counts is scored by how hard it was, and repeating the same easy thing earns less and less.

- Difficulty, not count. A scoped hit is worth more the further it is, the higher or lower you are
  than the target, the further your aim is off the street's line, and the faster the target moves.
  A hit straight down a sidewalk at 30 m on someone standing still is worth almost nothing.
- Variety. The same target, spot or action within a few minutes pays less each time (half per
  repeat, recovering over an hour). This stops farming without a daily cap.
- The server judges, never the client. It already flies every round, so it knows the range, the
  height, the angle and whether the scope was up.
- Each category has its own tiers. Your overall level is the sum, so a homemaker and a sniper both
  climb.
- Each badge is announced once, by speech and a sound, and listed in `/profile` and `/badges`.

### Categories

1. **Marksmanship**: pistol, rifle, shotgun, scoped; a track for each.
2. **Close combat**: melee and swords, when the sword exists.
3. **Driving**: cars, buses (stops served), motorbikes, trucks; later helicopters and trains. Clean
   driving counts: no collisions, stopping at crossings, parking.
4. **Exploration**: named places found, maps visited, distance on foot, roofs reached, every stop on
   a route.
5. **Building and crafting**: pieces placed, structures other players use, materials gathered.
6. **Domestic**: cooking, cleaning, laundry, shopping, gardening, repairs at home.
7. **Community**: helping new players, giving things away, returning lost items, guiding someone to
   a place, first aid.
8. **Commerce**: trading, banking, running a shop, deliveries, mail.
9. **Trades**: mechanic, electrician, plumber, locksmith, when those jobs exist.
10. **Outdoors**: fishing, hunting, birdwatching (the birds already have species), gardening.

### Tiers

Five tiers per category, each with its own title. A point is one ordinary action at ordinary
difficulty.

| Tier | Points | Marksmanship (scoped) | Domestic | Community | Driving |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50 | Marksman | Helping Hand | Neighbour | Learner |
| 2 | 250 | Sharpshooter | Homemaker | Good Samaritan | Driver |
| 3 | 1,000 | Sniper | Domestic Engineer | Pillar of the Community | Professional Driver |
| 4 | 4,000 | Master Sniper | Head of Household | Guardian | Road Captain |
| 5 | 15,000 | Ghost | Domestic Legend | Saint of the City | Wheelman |

Above tier 1, each tier also has a condition that volume alone cannot meet. Scoped shooting:

- Sharpshooter: 25 hits from at least 10 m above or below the target.
- Sniper: 50 hits beyond 300 m, 25 of them on moving targets, 25 at bearings more than 15 degrees
  off any street.
- Master Sniper: one hit beyond 800 m; 100 hits where wind or drop had to be allowed for.
- Ghost: 50 hits where nobody near the target heard where the shot came from (the report reached
  them only as an echo, or not at all).

Domestic:

- Homemaker: cooked, cleaned and shopped, each at least 20 times.
- Domestic Engineer: kept a home clean for 4 weeks running; cooked 20 different recipes.
- Head of Household: a household of more than one player or NPC that you feed and keep.
- Domestic Legend: all of the above, and a home other players visit.

Community:

- Good Samaritan: 20 helps of at least 3 kinds (gave away, returned, guided, first aid).
- Pillar of the Community: helped 10 different players; a new player you guided reached tier 1 in
  anything.

Marksmanship, driving, exploration and community can start now: the server already sees those
actions. Domestic, commerce and trades wait for cooking, cleaning, shops, banks, mail and jobs.

## 4. The wider world

In order, each needing the one before:

1. **Money**: a ledger on the server (every payment is two entries, so money is never made or lost by
   a bug), a wallet per player, prices on items.
2. **Banks**: accounts, deposits and withdrawals, cash machines, and tellers as NPCs behind a
   counter, with their own lines.
3. **Shops and selling**: things to buy, and things to sell, including bodies (to a morgue or a
   funeral home), salvage and crafted goods.
4. **Mail**: post boxes, letters and parcels as items, a post office, delivery as a job.
5. **NPC lives**: each NPC with a home, a job and a day (sleep, work, shop, home), then households
   with children. The walkers today walk loops; this replaces the loop with a schedule.

## 6. Network traffic

Today each player receives about 480 moving things a tick, 30 times a second: about 5.7 Mbit/s.
Earshot on the city is the whole map, and everything that can move is sent in full every tick,
including parked cars, people standing still and bodies.

In order, safest first:

1. **Things at rest**: send a parked car or a standing person once, then once a second, until it
   moves. No change to what you hear. (Done.)
2. **Composite parts**: the parts of a moving bus are sent one by one on the reliable channel every
   tick; the client can place them from the bus itself. (Not needed; see below.)
3. **The stats line**: sent every tick, should be sent when it changes. (Done.)
4. **Smaller numbers**: speed as whole centimetres per second, rotation in 4 bytes instead of 8,
   wheels only when they change. (Done.)
5. **Rate by distance**: everything near you 30 times a second, a car 500 m away 5 times a second.
   The client must then interpolate each thing over its own interval and take Doppler from the
   speed sent, not from the difference between positions. Risk: a distant car's sound steps if done
   badly. Measured first.
6. **Only the change**: send each field only when it changed since the last state the client
   confirmed.
7. **Traffic on the client**: cars and walkers follow known routes; the client can run them from a
   route, a seed and a time, and the server sends only corrections and events (a car stopping, a
   collision). The largest saving, and the most work.
8. **Voice**: today every player's voice goes to everyone on the map at 64 kbit/s. Send it only to
   players within hearing range, and let the codec send nothing during silence.
9. **Compression** of each packet: small next to the others.

Steps 1 to 4 are done (2026-10-05, measured with scripted players on a local city server): 5.8 to
1.75 Mbit/s and 673 to 176 packets a second with three players; positions within 1 mm of the old
stream, with and without packet loss. Step 2 turned out not to be needed: composite parts already
went unreliably; parked cars' panels are now left out like anything else at rest.

What the rest would save, from the 1.35 Mbit/s that remains:
- Rate by distance (5 Hz beyond 150 m): 50 to 70 percent. The client needs its own interpolation
  delay per thing (about 300 ms at 5 Hz); train notch and tyre demand must come from the server for
  distant things, not from differences in speed.
- A byte budget per packet filled by priority (how loud it is, how long since it was sent): caps the
  worst case.
- Only the change, against what the client confirmed: about 30 percent.
- Traffic on the client: most of what remains (walkers are about 60 percent of what moves), and the
  most work; corrections would be heard as snaps if done badly.
- Parts placed from their parent: about 7 percent.
- Voice: each talker costs every listener on the map 64 kbit/s. Culling by distance (speech is under
  the city's own sound by 100 to 200 m), silence suppression (about half) and 32 kbit/s (close to
  transparent for speech) are all low risk.
- Compression: 10 to 15 percent.
