### Why it died: one mutant ate the machine

`/var/log/messages` shows three kernel OOM kills (20:09, 20:15, 23:09). Each time a single Stryker
test host had grown to 36-38 GB. By the third, swap was full (56 kB free), and the machine thrashed
until it was force-restarted at 23:24. Stryker's timeout is far too slow to stop a fast allocator.

Most likely culprit: `RaceLine.cs` `Resample` (copy line 324). Its loop `for (k = 0; k < count; k++)`
only appends to a `List<Vector3>` and never indexes by `k`, so `k++` → `k--` runs forever without
throwing. A `List<Vector3>` that grows until it hits the array limit peaks at 36 GiB, which matches
the kills. Every `new RaceLine` reaches it. Other loops of the same shape, all with no cap:

- `LaneRoutes.cs` `Append` and `Connector`: `k++` → `k--`, appending to a `List<Vector3>`.
- `LaneRoutes.cs` `BackTo` and `Shortest`: `d + len` → `d - len` makes the costs negative. The
  lane graph has cycles and the Dijkstra has no closed set or iteration cap, so its queue grows
  forever.
- `CarDoor.cs` `ButterworthBandPass` pole loop: `k++` → `k--`, appending to a `List<Complex>`.

This is a production robustness point too. Those loops take their counts from map geometry, and a
NaN or negative lane length would hang or exhaust the server the same way.

**Before any rerun:**
- Cap each process with `DOTNET_GCHeapHardLimit` in `run.sh`. A runaway mutant then throws
  OutOfMemoryException inside its own test host and is killed, instead of taking the machine down.
- Lower the concurrency from 10.
- Stryker.NET cannot resume a partial run, so Common starts from zero.

## Real bugs found

These were found by reading the code around clusters of survivors. Each was checked against current
main.

### High confidence

1. **`/draw <a name you don't carry>` takes entity 0** (`HandsService.cs:216-220`).
   - `carried.FirstOrDefault(...)` returns `default(Entity)` (all zeros) when nothing matches.
   - The guard is `item == Entity.Null`, and Arch's `Entity.Null` has id -1, so the guard never
     fires.
   - The code goes on to put entity id 0 (whatever the map spawned first) in the player's hands.
     A following `/drop` sets it down in front of them.
   - Any player with something on their back can trigger it with a typo.
   - Fix: `FindIndex`, or compare against `default`.
   - Test: stow an AKM, `/draw crowbar` is refused, and no other entity gains a `HeldComponent`.
2. **Disconnecting while carrying loses the items for good** (`Program.cs` `DespawnSession`).
   - `MoveToMap` unseats the player and drops everything before destroying the body. Disconnect
     only destroys the body.
   - Held and stowed items keep a `HeldComponent` that points at a dead holder, and `Nearest` skips
     held items, so nobody can ever pick them up again.
   - Their `ParentComponent` also points at the dead body's id. Once Arch reuses that id, the items
     ride on whatever entity gets it.
   - Fix: do the same unseat and drop in `DespawnSession`.
3. **"Have a good day." is said at night** (`PedestrianSpeech.cs:640`, `:761`).
   - The daytime gate adds `bye_have_a_good_day` only by day.
   - `Candidates` then adds every "bye" line that passes `AnyTime`. Its regex matches "nice day" and
     "beautiful day" but not "good day", so the line comes back in at any hour.
   - "How's your day going?" (greet) gets in the same way.
   - Fix: widen the regex.
   - Test: `Partings(night)` picked through `Candidates` never yields it.
4. **About 3 in 10 phone calls end without a goodbye** (`PedestrianSpeech.cs:360-382`).
   - The ring-off line fires only if a line check lands in the last 6 s of the call.
   - The next line is scheduled `length + Uniform(3, 10)` ahead, which often passes `CallUntil`. The
     call then just stops.
   - That path also skips the `NextCallCheck` reset, so a new call can start at once.
   - The 29 % comes from the triage agent's simulation and has not been checked.
   - Fix: cap `NextPhoneLine` at `CallUntil`, or make the last line due once the next one would
     pass it.
5. **A level crossing reopens on the train's front, not its back** (`CrossingSystem.cs:187-189`).
   - `past = head - at` is checked against `ClearMetres`. The comment above it, and `MapRepository`,
     say the tail must be clear.
   - The city's light rail is 55 m long and its clearance 35 m, so about 20 m of tram can still be on
     the road when the crossing reopens. Bells stop and `IsClosedAt` releases road traffic.
   - Today this is hidden by the 6 s minimum closed time while the train does more than about
     3.3 m/s.
   - Fix: subtract the set's length.
6. **Train sound sources never follow the track's height** (`RailSystem.cs:248-249`).
   - `height = t.Y - pos.Y; pos.Y += height` works out to `pos.Y = t.Y`, so the source keeps its old
     absolute height until it is 6 m off, then snaps to 0.5 m above the rail.
   - The city track is flat, so nobody hears this yet.
7. **A track with one stop holds its train at the platform forever** (`RailSystem.cs:161-185`).
   - `NextStop = (0 + 1) % 1 = 0`, and the train is still within 1.5 m at speed 0, so it starts
     dwelling again.
   - The city has two stops, so this is latent.

### Medium confidence

8. **Movement input lets NaN through** (`MessageDispatcher.cs:34`).
   - The only size check normalises when `Length() > 1`. A NaN length fails that comparison and
     passes through.
   - A hostile client could put a NaN position into the spatial grid. Not yet checked whether
     `SharedMovementEngine` catches it downstream.
9. **The give-way slow-down measures the wrong lane** (`VehicleSystem.cs:739-742`).
   - `JunctionHold` corrects the leg with `ShortOfTheLine`, but the caller recomputes `toLine` with
     plain `WhereOnLane`.
   - In exactly the case `ShortOfTheLine` exists for, `toLine` comes from the next lane and the
     speed cap stops capping. A car can speed up in the last metre or two before the line.
10. **The deadlock breaker can release two cars at once** (`Junctions.cs:155-163`).
    - It refuses only for a conflicting car that is inside, or coming at more than 0.5 m/s.
    - Two deadlocked cars that reach patience within about 0.25 s of each other both pull off.
    - That matches the `No_two_vehicles_meet_inside_a_junction` failure in todo.md: two nearly
      stopped cars 1.9 m apart. This is a hypothesis.

## Traffic safety has no working guard

The two baseline failures are `CrosswalkTests.Walkers_wait_for_a_gap...` and
`CarFollowingTests.No_two_vehicles_meet_inside_a_junction`. todo.md has them under "faults that come
and go with the mix". 189 surviving mutants are covered by one of these two tests, and usually by
nothing else.

So with every passing test green, each of these survives:
- walkers crossing only when traffic is coming (`Crosswalks.cs:222`);
- a walker stepping out with a car on the crossing (`:247`);
- walkers never stopping at the kerb (`VehicleSystem.cs:566`);
- junctions seeing nobody, so nobody waits (`Following.cs:41`);
- not waiting for a car already inside a junction (`Junctions.cs:122`);
- the 09-28 long-truck fix being turned off (`Junctions.cs:187`).

**Recommendation:** small deterministic scenes alongside the city-wide tests: one car, one walker,
one crossing; two cars on crossing paths; a priority road against a side road. They pin the rules
whatever the traffic mix does. This is worth more than any other test work in this list.
