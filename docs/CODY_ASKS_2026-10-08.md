## 1. One continuous world

Agreed: walking off one place into the next is continuous. Real places are built once by the server
from the open data (OpenStreetMap, the elevation survey, soil survey), and the built tiles are stored on
the server. The raw data is never kept. Players are always sent tiles as they move (that is streaming,
and it happens either way), but a stored tile is read from disk instead of being built again, which
removes the slow step: fetching and building. Weather is the same: the server fetches it, keeps the
result, and sends players what they need.

- One world, not a map per state or city. States, counties, cities and towns are names on the world
  (from the data), used for "where am I" and for travel, not separate files.
- The map list gets two parts: **The world** (real places; pick a town or an address to arrive at) and
  **Maps** (game and player-made maps: the city, the speedway, anything built with the editor). A player
  map can later be placed somewhere in the world, but it is a separate list.
- Coordinates: inside the game they stay (metres on the UTM grid; everything needs them). For players
  they are not useful, so "where am I" says the nearest address, the street, the town and the state, and
  what is near ("outside 1042 Belmont Avenue, Albany, Oregon, facing north on Belmont"). Builders still
  get coordinates in the editor, and latitude and longitude on request.
- Docs: docs/WORLD_STREAMING.md stage 2 (one world) and stage 3 (seamless travel) cover this; this
  section is the agreed shape.

## 3. Who builds the tiles: server or client

Server (Cody's lean, and the convention for shared worlds).

- For the server: one copy of the truth, so every player hears and collides with the same world; built
  once and shared by everyone who goes there; clients stay light (a phone or an old laptop could play);
  nothing a modified client builds can be trusted for collision or bullets anyway.
- Against: the server pays the CPU and the disk; the first visit to a new place waits for the build.
- How to make the wait disappear: build ahead in the background along the direction of travel; pre-build
  the places people use (towns players live in); and an offline tool on the desk machine can pre-build a
  whole state and upload it.
- Disk: a medium-detail tile is tens to a few hundred kilobytes compressed; a whole state is gigabytes,
  fine on the VPS's 134 GB free. Cody 2026-10-09: the world store's disk cap starts at 20 GB, a setting he can change.

## 4. Sending distant things less often

Agreed with Cody's condition: only where it costs no realism. The plan sends a distant thing's position
less often (5 times a second beyond 150 m) but with its velocity, so the client moves it smoothly between
updates; Doppler and a train's or engine's speed come from the server's own numbers, not from guessing
between positions. It is accepted only if a test shows no audible difference: bearing error under
1 degree, pitch error under 0.5 % (below what an ear notices), and no steps in a pass-by. Future graphics
interpolate the same way. If the test fails, it is not done.

## 5. Sound from geometry, not from labels

Cody's point is right: physically there is no difference between a wall, a floor and a ceiling. A
surface is a surface: its material, its thickness, its shape and its angle decide what it does to sound.
A plank turned 30 degrees is just a plank at 30 degrees. The labels exist for historical reasons: the
game started with boxes, and the first acoustic code took shortcuts (a floor slab counted as a barrier,
reverb chosen by a named region, sound routed between rooms by authored portals).

Where it stands now:
- Reflections already come from any surface at any angle: Steam Audio traces the triangles themselves.
- Doors are already geometry (since 2026-09-30): the leaf swings and the scene is rebuilt with it, and
  sound bends round the jambs as routes.
- Regions remain for two things: choosing which reverb you are in, and naming places for speech.
  Portals remain as a fast way to route sound through openings.

The direction: let the materials and geometry speak for themselves.
- Rooms from geometry: work out "how enclosed am I and by what" from the surfaces around the listener
  (partly there: regions measure themselves, the openness boundary). Then regions become only names.
- Routing from geometry: find openings from the geometry (partly there: openings from gaps in faces)
  instead of authored portals.
- Transmission per construction, not per label: a floor is two layers in contact, so it must count as
  one layered panel, not two barriers. That is the cause of "upstairs 15 dB too quiet in the lows", and
  it is the first fix in this area.

Why ground and ceiling reflections were a problem: a perfect mirror gives an exact copy of the sound a
few milliseconds late, and an exact copy combs (flanging, the "phased" sound). Real surfaces are rough
and real heads move, so real reflections are smeared. The fix was never to remove them but to make them
diffuse (done for engines and echoes, still open for some paths).

Realistic aspects still missing:
- Sound through structure: footsteps upstairs heard through the ceiling, a door slammed two rooms away.
- Flanking: sound going round a wall through the floor or ceiling instead of through it.
- Diffraction over any edge (today: box edges and door jambs).
- Small rooms' own resonances (room modes) below about 200 Hz.
- People and furniture absorbing sound.
- Outdoors far away: wind and temperature bending sound up or down, and turbulence making far sound waver.

**(Cody)** Order: floors first (the 15 dB), then rooms from geometry, then structure-borne sound.

## 6. Enemies and Dinosaur World

Enemies are characters that can hurt you and be killed: an NPC with health, weapons or attacks, and a
reason to fight.

Dinosaur World, the first enemy map:
- Habitat: Late Cretaceous (Hell Creek, about 66 million years ago): conifers, ferns, cycads, rivers and
  swamps, no grass, insects and frogs, a warm wet climate.
- Animals, at their real sizes: Tyrannosaurus (about 8 tonnes, 12 m), Triceratops (6-9 t),
  Edmontosaurus and Parasaurolophus (3-4 t, herds), Ankylosaurus (5-8 t), Dakotaraptor (a large
  feathered raptor, about 5 m), Quetzalcoatlus (pterosaur, 10 m wingspan), and a sauropod
  (Alamosaurus, 30-40 t) for scale.
- Sounds, modelled, not recorded:
  - Footsteps from mass, foot area and gait: a sauropod's footfall is mostly below 50 Hz, felt more than
    heard, and the ground carries it; a T. rex's is a heavy thud with the ground's own ring; small
    raptors patter.
  - Calls: the evidence (birds and crocodilians, their closest relatives) points to closed-mouth booming
    and low hoots through the throat, not movie roars. Parasaurolophus's crest is a resonating tube
    whose length gives its notes. Large animals' calls are very low.
  - Breathing, body movement, teeth, crashing through plants.
- Weapons and difficulty, from real ballistics: small arms barely matter to a T. rex; a large-calibre
  rifle (.458, .505) needs vital hits; a raptor falls to a rifle or shotgun but is fast and hunts in a
  group. A table per animal and weapon, from penetration and the animal's size, sets difficulty.
- Enemy scan: an option that announces enemies near you on its own (kind, distance, bearing, moving
  closer or away), built on the narration. On Dinosaur World it would be on by default.
- Rings: players, NPCs and enemies each get their own ring in the Shift-comma and Shift-period cycle.

## 7. Railways, and the editor's words

- Placing a line: a track is a path of points with a grade. The editor needs a track tool: lay points
  as you walk or by typing them, choose surface, elevated or underground, and add stations and
  crossings. An underground line makes its own tunnel (a bored tube or a cut-and-cover box) through the
  ground, with no digging needed; stations open to the street with stairs.
- A train across several maps, or across the world, needs the one continuous world (item 1). Within one
  map, the track tool is enough.
- Typing values: today a field the menu calls "type" puts the command on the command line for you to
  finish. Cody's idea is better: an editor dialog with a labelled text box showing the current value, its
  unit and range, Enter to apply and Escape to cancel, read by Orca and NVDA as a normal text field.
- Words that confuse:
  - The **library** is every model the game knows, shared by all maps: engines, vehicles, machines (air
    conditioners, mowers), water, fire, trees, horns, bells, trains, and prefabs.
  - A **prefab** is a placeable object (its shape, material and which model makes its sound). A
    **model** is the physics and sound recipe. A car prefab points to a car model.
  - Air conditioners and car engines are separate kinds because they are different models.
  - "Places" and "Places and rooms" overlap: merge them into one list.
  An editor wording and layout review comes with the dialog work.

## 12. Weather as a system

- Today: `/weather` sets wind and rain by hand, or `auto` picks them, and setting rain also resets wind.
- The plan: a weather model with real systems moving across the world: high and low pressure, warm and
  cold fronts, squall lines, supercells with rotation, sea breezes. Wind, cloud, temperature, humidity
  and precipitation all follow from it, so they belong together. On top of that, every value can be held
  by hand on its own (temperature, cloud cover, wind speed and direction and gusts, precipitation type,
  rate and particle size) without touching the others. "Overcast and windy, no rain" is then just two
  held values.
- Drop size: real raindrops break up above about 5-6 mm, so 5 mm is close to the largest possible.
  Normally drop size follows from the rate (Marshall-Palmer: heavier rain, bigger drops). Setting it
  apart from the rate stays possible as an override.
- Particle size really depends on the type: sleet pellets under 5 mm, snowflakes 2-20 mm (larger
  aggregates in wet snow), hail 5 mm to over 100 mm. So one "size" control, with the range of the
  current type.
- Rain on cars and roofs: rain on a roof you are not under is still heard from outside, as in reality;
  whether each surface is heard at the right level is to be checked with a capture.
